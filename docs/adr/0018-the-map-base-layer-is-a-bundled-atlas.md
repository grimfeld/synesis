# The Map's base layer is a bundled atlas, with online detail only up close

The Map drew Places over the standard OpenStreetMap tiles: motorways, national borders and modern city names in saturated colours, deaf to the Skin and dark mode, and needing the network in a local-first app. We decided the base layer is a shaded-relief atlas with no modern labels or roads, bundled with the app: Natural Earth land, lakes and rivers (public domain) as shapes the app fills with Skin tokens, and a hillshade of the Bible's world (Rome to Persia, the Black Sea to Sheba) at zooms 3–7, blended onto them. Dark mode gets a dark atlas, and any Skin can recolour land and sea. From zoom 8, where the bundle runs out of detail, a muted online layer draws over it when there is a network; offline, the atlas simply goes on at zoom 7's detail.

**Amended at build (2026-09-28).** Natural Earth's raster relief could not be fetched, and at about 1.8 km a pixel it would have been soft past zoom 6 anyway. The hillshade is computed instead from Mapzen's Terrain Tiles (AWS Open Data: SRTM, GMTED2010, ETOPO1, all public domain, attribution "Mapzen Terrain Tiles"), masked to Natural Earth's coastline so the Jordan valley's below-sea-level land is not mistaken for sea. Zoom 8 was dropped rather than added: zooms 3–7 come to 9 MB of greyscale tiles plus 434 KB of shapes, and zoom 8 alone would have been about four times zoom 7.

A Bible map should look like a Bible atlas, not a navigation app, and it should work on a plane. Close-up detail (Gethsemane against the Temple) is rare enough to be allowed to depend on the network.

## Considered options

- **Keep OpenStreetMap's standard tiles.** Free and already built, and rejected: modern labels on an ancient map, no theming, online only, and OSM's tile usage policy does not cover an app shipped to many users.
- **A muted online style** (light and dark "Positron"-style tiles). Much of the visual gain for little work, and rejected because it keeps modern labels, the network dependency and a third party's terms.
- **The bundled atlas alone.** Fully offline, and rejected because the Map would go blurry at exactly the zoom where two Places in one city need telling apart.

## Consequences

- The app grows by the tile set: 9 MB of relief (zooms 3–7) and 434 KB of shapes, measured.
- The atlas is regenerated by `scripts/atlas.mjs` (Natural Earth vectors and Terrain Tiles, cached in `target/atlas-cache`) rather than hand-kept; its output is committed, so no build or CI run needs the network.
- The online layer is still OpenStreetMap's standard tiles, now only from zoom 8. Whether its usage policy covers that, or another provider should serve it, is open (PLAN §27).
- Tests keep relying on `data-zoom` / `data-center` rather than on tiles, which are now local but still pictures.
