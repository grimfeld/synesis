# Two documents side by side

Reading one document while writing another needs no going back and forth: a window shows up to two Panes side by side, and each goes back and forward on its own. The side panel, the Command Palette and Back follow the Pane you used last, marked by a thin line along its top.

1. Open the Note you are writing with [Search](command:nav.search). Hold Cmd (Ctrl on Windows and Linux) and click a link in it: what it names opens in a second Pane on the right, and the Note stays on the left. [Open in other Pane](command:pane.open_other) does the same for the whole page you are on.
2. Click into the left Pane. The thin line moves to it, and the side panel now describes its document. Open something else there, then press [Back](command:nav.back): only the left Pane goes back, and the right one keeps what it shows.
3. Drag the divider between the two Panes to give one of them more room. Click the divider and the arrow keys move it too. Each Pane keeps at least 320 pixels.
4. Run [Swap Panes](command:pane.swap) to put the document you write in on the other side. Each keeps its own history and its width.
5. Try it once: in a Note of your own, Cmd-click or Ctrl-click a link to read what it names beside your text, then run [Close Pane](command:pane.close) in the Pane you no longer need. The other one stays.

## Good to know

- Two Panes need room for both. On a phone there is only ever one Pane, and a Cmd-click or Ctrl-click opens a link as a plain click would. In a window too narrow for both, only the Pane you used last shows; widen the window, or close the sidebar or the side panel, and the other comes back.
- A Composition's talk and Board can still sit side by side inside a Pane, given the room.
- When two Panes are open, they come back as they were the next time you open this Vault on this Device. One Pane opens on Home, as always.
- While two Panes show, each has its own **×** in its header, which closes it.
- The same document can be open in both Panes. What you type in one appears in the other at once, and the other keeps its own place in the text.
