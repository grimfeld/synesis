# Map & Journeys

The Map shows every Place in your Vault that has coordinates, on an atlas of the Bible's world, and draws your Journeys as routes through their Stops. Use it to see where the things you study happened, what kind of places they were, and which Places a Book or a journey takes you through.

![The Map: Places as pins shaped by their kind, on the atlas](image:map)

1. Open [Go to Map](command:nav.map). Each Place with coordinates is a pin whose shape says what kind of place it is: a settlement, a mountain, water or a site; a region is an area, filled in where it has a Border and shown by its name alone where it has none. The **Legend** in the corner lists the kinds on screen. Click a pin to open its Hub.
2. Narrow the Places: type in **Search Places**, or open **Filter** to choose Tags, the Books a Place is **Mentioned in**, or **Only Places something mentions**. **Clear filters** brings every Place back.
3. In **Filter**, **Colour by** chooses what the pins' colour tells you: **Kind** (the default), **Tag**, **Book** or **None**. With Tag or Book, pick up to five to compare: each gets a colour, a Place in two of them gets a split pin, and every other Place turns grey. The ✕ beside a value in the **Legend** drops it from the comparison.
4. In **Filter**, turn on a Journey under **Journeys**: its route is drawn in travel order, in its own colour, with each Stop numbered on the edge of its pin and arrows for the direction. A Place visited twice carries both numbers. On a Journey's Hub, **Show on map** does the same, and the **Stops** list says which Stops cannot be drawn.
5. Narrow the Map to a Period: click **Period** under the header and type the first and last years, say 1100 and 1000 BCE, or drag the slider beneath it. Its two ends widen or narrow the Period, and the bar between them moves it whole. The Map then shows the Places that existed in those years, the Journeys made in them, and each Event of those years as a small diamond beside its Place's pin; click one to open the Event. Click a pin while a Period is set and its card lists the **Characters here** in those years: Jerusalem's lists David. **Places**, on the same row, draws only the Places you pick. The Timeline shares the Period and the Places: set them on one, and the other shows the same. **Clear Period** brings everything back.
6. A Place's kind is chosen on its Hub under **Kind**: one of the five that come with the app, or **New kind…** to make your own from a name and an icon. A Place picked from the list of Bible places gets its kind with its coordinates; for Places you made before, [Fill in Place kinds from the gazetteer](command:map.fill_kinds) names each one it would change and asks first.
7. A Place without coordinates is not on the Map. Open its Hub and press **Set location**: pick the place from the list of Bible places that comes with the app, by its English name, or click the map, then press **Use this location**.
8. A region can have a **Border**. Open Egypt's Hub and press **Draw Border** under **Border**, or run [Draw Border](command:map.draw_border) and pick the region on the Map. Click to place each corner, drag a corner to move it, and double-click to close: the region is drawn as a filled area in its colour. **Edit Border** on the Hub brings the corners back to move and **Save Border**; **Delete Border** removes it, and the region is shown by its name again. To reuse a published historical map instead, press **Import Border** on the region's Hub, or run [Import Border](command:map.import_border) from it, and pick a `.geojson` file: it is copied beside the Place with every piece it has, islands included. A file with no area in it is refused, and the message says why.
9. A region with a Border stands for every Place inside it. Under **Places** choose Judah, whose Border the demo Vault already has, and set the Period to 640–580 BCE: the Map draws Judah's Border and only the Places inside it, Jerusalem, Bethlehem and Anathoth among them, and Corinth is gone. Move the Period to Paul's day, 30–60 CE, and Judah's Border is hidden and its Places go with it: the kingdom had ended, and a region's Border is shown, and contains, only in the years of its own Dates. **Clear Period** and the ✕ on Judah bring everything back.
10. Try it once: create a Journey you are studying with [New Journey](command:create.journey). On its Hub, press **Add a Stop** and type the first Place: your own Places are offered first, then Bible places, which become Places of yours, coordinates and kind included, when you pick one. Add each Stop in turn, fix the order with the arrows, then press **Show on map**: the route is drawn through your Stops. If it was only a test, the delete button in its Hub's header removes the Journey; Places it created stay, and are removed the same way.

## Good to know

- A Place's kind is its **Kind** Property, plain text that Obsidian shows as it is. Kinds you make are kept in the Vault, so your other devices draw the same pins; deleting one in Settings leaves your Places as they are, with the plain pin.
- A Journey's Stops are the links in its **Places** Property, in travel order, so Obsidian shows and edits the same list.
- A Place with no Dates is never left out by a Period, and neither is a Journey. An Event with no Date is left out, and the Period row says how many. The Tags chosen on the Timeline narrow the Map too: they keep a Place that carries one, or where a Character or Event carrying one is in the Period. The Map's own Tags, in **Filter**, narrow its Places further.
- **Colour by**, the Books filter and **Only Places something mentions** are remembered; the search, Tags, the values you compare and Journeys last until you close the app.
- Which Places lie inside a region is decided by its Border alone, and worked out again whenever the Border or a Place's coordinates change. A Place on the line counts as inside; a region with no Border contains nothing.
- A Border is a `.geojson` file kept beside its Place and named in its **Border** Property, so Obsidian and map tools open it as it is. Pairing and iCloud carry it to your other devices. If the file cannot be drawn, the Place's Hub says why.
- The atlas comes with the app and works offline, down to the scale of a region. Closer in, a street map from OpenStreetMap takes over when you are online; your pins and routes never need a connection.
