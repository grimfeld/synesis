# Properties & Dates

Properties are the named values a document carries besides its text: a Place's latitude, a Note's Source, a Character's birth. Dates among them are written the way you would say them, and the right pair puts a page on the Timeline.

1. Open one of your Places or Characters, or create one with [New Place](command:create.place). Its Properties sit in the card at the top of its page (beside a Note, under **Properties** in the side panel), each with a field that suits its type: a number, a link, a Date.
2. Type a name into **Add property** and press Enter. The first time a name is used you choose its type, and the whole Vault keeps it: type the name on another page and its type is already known. Click a Property's label to change its type or **Remove property**.
3. Write Dates the way a reader says them: `c. 1513 BCE`, `14 Nisan 33 CE`, `52 CE`. The app reads the year, how precise it is and whether it is approximate, and never rewrites what you typed. A Date it cannot read is flagged in red under **Dates** on its page, and stays off the Timeline.
4. Try it once: create a Character with [New Character](command:create.character) and fill in **Born** and **Died**. Run [Go to Timeline](command:nav.timeline): the two Dates make a Span, drawn as a bar on the Character's own Lane. An Event's **Start** and **End** work the same way. Delete the Character from its page header if it was only practice.

## Good to know

- Choose **Date (Bible)** for Scripture chronology and **Calendar date** for today's world.
- A Span is never declared: any page with `born` and `died`, or `start` and `end`, has one. Any other Date is a single mark on its Lane.
- Property names stay in English in the file (`born`, `died`), whatever the app's language, so the Vault reads the same in Obsidian.
